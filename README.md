
# BCEN Certified Emergency Nurse (CEN) Certification Exam Guide

[![BCEN CEN Certification](https://img.shields.io/badge/BCEN-CEN-005A9C?style=for-the-badge)](https://bcen.org/cen/)
[![Certification Type](https://img.shields.io/badge/Type-Specialty%20Nursing%20Certification-555555?style=for-the-badge)](https://bcen.org/cen/)

A dedicated study guide and clinical reference repository for nurses preparing for the **Board of Certification for Emergency Nursing (BCEN) Certified Emergency Nurse (CEN) Certification Exam**, covering emergency assessment, acute patient care, clinical decision-making, and professional nursing practice.

---

## Exam Overview

The CEN certification validates specialized knowledge and clinical expertise in emergency nursing, including the assessment and management of patients with a wide range of acute and critical conditions.

- **Exam Format**: Computer-based multiple-choice examination, delivered at a PSI testing center or through live remote proctoring.
- **Target Audience**: Registered nurses working in emergency departments and professionals seeking to demonstrate specialized emergency nursing knowledge.
- **Prerequisites**: A current, unrestricted RN license in the United States, a U.S. territory, Canada, Australia, or an equivalent license. International candidates may need credential evaluation. Two years of emergency nursing experience is recommended but not required.

## Core Exam Domains & Knowledge Areas

### 1. Cardiovascular Emergencies

- Assess and manage acute coronary syndromes, dysrhythmias, and cardiopulmonary arrest.
- Recognize heart failure, hypertension, aneurysms, and vascular emergencies.
- Identify cardiogenic and obstructive shock, cardiac trauma, and thromboembolic disease.

### 2. Respiratory Emergencies

- Assess asthma, COPD, respiratory infections, and airway obstruction.
- Recognize pneumothorax, pulmonary embolism, and pulmonary edema.
- Manage inhalation injuries, respiratory trauma, and acute respiratory distress.

### 3. Neurological Emergencies

- Assess neurological disorders, headaches, and increased intracranial pressure.
- Recognize seizures, stroke, transient ischemic attacks, and meningitis.
- Manage head and spinal cord trauma and neurogenic shock.

### 4. Gastrointestinal Emergencies

- Assess gastrointestinal bleeding, obstruction, perforation, and appendicitis.
- Recognize pancreatitis, hepatitis, cirrhosis, and gallbladder disorders.
- Evaluate gastrointestinal trauma and other acute abdominal conditions.

### 5. Genitourinary, Gynecology, and Obstetrical Emergencies

- Recognize renal failure, urinary retention, renal calculi, and genitourinary trauma.
- Assess gynecological infections, ovarian disorders, and sexual assault.
- Manage obstetrical emergencies, including ectopic pregnancy, hemorrhage, and preeclampsia.

### 6. Mental Health Emergencies

- Assess anxiety, depression, bipolar disorder, and psychotic disorders.
- Recognize suicidal and homicidal ideation and intentional overdose.
- Manage aggressive, violent, and other acute behavioral emergencies.

### 7. Medical Emergencies

- Assess allergic reactions, anaphylaxis, and hematologic disorders.
- Recognize electrolyte imbalances, endocrine disorders, and immunocompromise.
- Manage sepsis, shock, substance-related emergencies, and withdrawal.

### 8. Musculoskeletal and Wound Emergencies

- Assess fractures, dislocations, ligament injuries, and compartment syndrome.
- Recognize amputations, osteomyelitis, and other musculoskeletal conditions.
- Manage wounds, lacerations, penetrating injuries, and wound bleeding.

### 9. Head, Eye, Ear, Nose, Throat Emergencies

- Assess facial nerve disorders, maxillofacial infections, and trauma.
- Recognize ocular injuries, retinal disorders, and increased intraocular pressure.
- Evaluate ear, nose, and throat emergencies, including epistaxis and epiglottitis.

### 10. Environment and Toxicology Emergencies, and Communicable Diseases

- Manage burns, chemical exposure, electrical injuries, and temperature-related emergencies.
- Recognize toxic exposures, including acids, alkalis, carbon monoxide, and cyanide.
- Identify communicable diseases, including respiratory viruses and multidrug-resistant infections.

### 11. Professional Issues

- Apply triage, prioritization, mass-casualty, and patient-throughput principles.
- Address legal and ethical issues, consent, continuity of care, and end-of-life decisions.
- Recognize abuse, neglect, workplace violence, and impaired nursing practice.
